## TL;DR
- A free, **five-level maturity model** Pistoia launched in Nov 2020 to help life sciences organizations assess how mature their UX practice is and plan next steps.
- It assesses UX across a small set of **dimensions**: Impact and Process (confirmed), plus UX Metrics & Analytics (per R&D World) **[verify count]**.
- At the top of the scale, **UX is part of company strategy and business value**. One level below, UX is embedded in every project delivery team from the start.

## What the source says
- Launched **18 Nov 2020** through the UXLS community. ([Pistoia release](https://www.pistoiaalliance.org/news/pistoia-alliance-launches-user-experience-maturity-model/))
- **Five stages per dimension**, level 1 (low) to level 5 (high). (Pistoia release)
- **Dimensions:** Impact, UX Metrics and Analytics, and Process, per [R&D World](https://www.rdworldonline.com/the-pistoia-alliance-launches-user-experience-maturity-model-for-life-sciences/). Pistoia's excerpt confirms Impact and Process. **[verify]**
- Described as a simple framework and tabular guide for assessing how UX strategy is integrated and the impact it has. ([UXLS community](https://pistoiaalliance.org/community/uxls/))

## Our read

**Why a 2020 UX model belongs in an AI PMF library:** UX maturity is a strong predictor of whether an organization can absorb AI. Low-maturity orgs bolt AI onto broken workflows ("Start with the workflow, not the AI": [S001](S001-pistoia-ux-for-ai-and-ai-for-ux.md)). High-maturity orgs can design the role AI plays. That makes this model a **qualification lens**: it tells you which accounts are ready to buy AI-in-workflow and which need foundational work first.

**Pairing idea:** Pistoia also publishes a **FAIR Maturity Matrix** for data. Plot accounts on **data maturity (FAIR) × UX maturity (UXLS)** and you get a four-box **AI readiness map**:
- High data / high UX → ready for workflow-embedded AI (frontier lab + services co-sell)
- High data / low UX → AI gets built but not adopted (services: workflow and role design)
- Low data / high UX → great prototypes that can't scale (services: data foundation, e.g., IDMP-O)
- Low data / low UX → foundational transformation first

**So what for services:** A credible, buyer-built maturity model is a gift for discovery. Run a lightweight UXLS + FAIR assessment as the paid first step. It's a low-risk entry offer that naturally sizes the follow-on.

**So what for frontier labs:** Use it to tier enterprise accounts. Workflow-embedded deployments will stick at level 4–5 orgs; lower-maturity orgs need partners to get there.

**Job to be done (Head of Digital / UX in pharma R&D):** When leadership asks why our AI pilots aren't being adopted, I want an objective, industry-recognized way to show where our practice stands, so I can make the case for investing in workflow and UX, not just more models.

| Current state | Future state | Gap (the offer) |
|---|---|---|
| UX applied project by project; AI pilots launched without workflow redesign | UX embedded in delivery from day one; AI roles designed with users | AI-readiness assessment (UXLS + FAIR); role-design sprints |
